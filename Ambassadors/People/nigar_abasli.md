## 👤 Biography
I got into cybersecurity the way most people do — curiosity that slowly became an obsession. I wanted to understand how things break, not just how they work. That led me down the usual rabbit holes: CTFs, HTB, bug bounty, eventually low-level stuff, reverse engineering, and somewhere along the way it stopped being a hobby and started feeling like the thing I actually want to do.
 
Right now I am focused on penetration testing and offensive security. I practice actively, I read a lot, I break things in lab environments, and I try to be honest about what I know versus what I am still figuring out. Currently grinding toward the CPTS certification, which has been a good forcing function for tightening up my methodology.
 
I joined NTHW because I think this space benefits most from people who are still in the middle of learning and are willing to share that process honestly — not just the wins, but the dead ends and the "why didn't I think of that" moments. That is the kind of community I want to be part of, and contribute to.

## 🛠️ Technical Competencies and Interests

My main interest is offensive security — web and API pentesting, network enumeration, privilege escalation, and the methodology that ties it all together. I practice on HTB and bug bounty platforms, and I try to approach every target with a consistent process rather than just throwing tools at it. Manual testing with Burp Suite is where I spend most of my time on the web side, focused on OWASP Top 10 and beyond.
 
I have a genuine interest in the low-level side of security — binary exploitation, memory internals, how vulnerabilities actually manifest at the system level. I am not an expert in it yet, but it is the direction I am heading. My background in C/C++ and x86 Assembly gives me a foundation to reason about memory, pointers, and program behavior in ways that I think make me a better tester overall — even when I am working on something higher-level like a web app.
 
I also have a working understanding of application security from the defensive side — how SAST and DAST tools work under the hood, where they fall short, and what manual review catches that automated scanning misses. That context is useful when I am doing offensive work because I understand what defenders are looking at.

## 🚀 Planned Initiatives as NTHW Ambassador

- **Mini-projects:**
  - *"Vulnerability of the Week" Series* — Recurring write-ups breaking down a real-world CVE or OWASP Top 10 vulnerability: what it is, how it is exploited, and how to remediate it. Digestible, technical, and practically useful.
  - *"How I Pwned It" Writeup Series* — HTB/CTF machine writeups published post-retirement, walking through my exact methodology: recon, enumeration, exploitation, and post-exploitation — including what failed before what worked.
  - *Methodology Breakdown Sessions* — Deep dives into a single pentesting phase per session (e.g., subdomain enumeration, Linux privilege escalation patterns, Active Directory attack paths). Granular and repeatable.
  - *Live Bug Bounty Walkthrough Sessions* — Sanitized walkthroughs of real bug bounty findings, covering the thought process from discovery to report — DEF CON-style, scaled for community members.
 
- **Community:** Active participation in discussions, answering technical questions, supporting members who are early in their learning journey, and co-maintaining resource lists relevant to pentesting and AppSec.
 
- **Content:** Technical write-ups, vulnerability explainers, and honest beginner guides — accurate and in-depth rather than surface-level. Focus on offensive security, web/API testing, and the realities of working in security professionally.
 
- **Events:** Coordinating CTF sprints, CPTS/OSCP study group sessions, and community-driven pentesting practice labs. Interested in organizing informal "office hours" where members can bring real questions and blockers and get honest, experienced answers.
